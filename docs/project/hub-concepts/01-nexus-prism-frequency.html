Four ways into the worlds

Each preview is a working sketch of one hub concept: what you see when you arrive, how you move between the four worlds, and the hand-off when you step into one. These are rough motion studies, not the final build. The real site would add the static and reduced-motion versions.

Concept A+B

Nexus in prism light

The Nexus structure (a ring of gates you drift around) drawn in the prism's light instead of game UI. Calm, iridescent, still clearly a place you travel through.

Arrive
One white thread of light draws across the dark, splits into four colours, and each colour curls into a gate.
The hub
Four light-rings in a slow orbit. Scroll, drag or arrow keys bring one to the front. Writing, About and Connect are quieter rooms below the ring.
Leave
The front gate widens until its colour fills the screen; the world fades up from that colour, so the jump between sites feels continuous.
Return
Every world keeps one small "back to the Nexus" light. The hub replays the gate closing behind you, with that world now facing you.

Trade-off: less overtly "game" than A. The quest (hidden keys, sealed fifth gate) still fits on top.

Concept A

The Nexus

The most Ready Player One of the four: a boot sequence, a visitor pass, a HUD, and gates with their own physical motifs.

Arrive
Black screen, a cursor, a short boot log: link established, visitor pass issued, your local time read as "coordinates". Shown once per visit; skippable.
The hub
A ring of gates in a void with a horizon grid. Each gate animates its world: shutter blades, standing waves, drifting cloud lines, sparks on a blueprint.
Leave
A warp: the grid streaks, the gate rushes at you, a hard cut to the world's colour, then the world loads.
Quest
A key hidden in each world. All four open a sealed fifth gate. Progress lives only in the visitor's browser.
ExposureResonanceAltitudeForge

Trade-off: strongest "experience", but the HUD and boot text can feel like a game menu and need careful restraint to stay in Holominimal's minimal register.

Concept B

The Prism

No ring, no HUD. One beam of white light hits a slowly turning prism and splits into four. Each beam is a world.

Arrive
A single beam crosses the page, finds the prism, and the spectrum fans out with your name in the refracted light.
The hub
Four coloured beams run off the right edge. Hovering one makes it carry its world's texture: grain, a waveform, cloud, a grid.
Leave
The chosen beam widens until it is the whole screen. The world begins inside that colour.
Return
The world's colour narrows back into a beam and slides home into the prism.
ExposureResonanceAltitudeForge

Trade-off: the most elegant and the most "Holominimal", but less of a place to explore; the portal feeling is lighter.

Concept C

Frequency Dial

The hub as a receiver caught between stations. You tune through static; each world is a station that locks in with a clean signal.

Arrive
The page opens on soft static and a needle. The first station drifts in by itself so the idea is clear.
The hub
Scroll or drag to tune. Between stations: noise. Near one: its colour and name resolve out of the static.
Leave
"Tune in" locks the signal; the static clears completely and the world takes over.
Return
Static rushes back in for a beat, then you are on the dial again, still on that world's station.

Trade-off: the most distinctive interaction, but more "receiver" than "portal", and static is tiring if it is loud. Fits the music world best of all.
